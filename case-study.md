# Case Study - Acute urticarial reaction following diclofenac initiation

## Case status
Fictional educational training case.

## Patient profile
29-year-old man with intermittent mechanical back pain.

## Medication timeline
- Pantoprazole 40 mg once daily: long-standing therapy.
- Diclofenac 50 mg twice daily: newly initiated for acute back pain.
- Approximately 48 hours later: itchy raised red rash over trunk and upper limbs with mild eyelid puffiness.
- Diclofenac discontinued: symptoms begin to settle within 24 hours.
- By Day 4: symptoms almost completely resolved.
- Rechallenge: not performed.

## Initial ADR assessment
**Suspected medicine:** Diclofenac

**Reaction:** Generalized pruritic urticarial rash with mild eyelid puffiness.

**Temporal relationship:** Compatible with a reaction beginning about two days after initiation.

**Dechallenge:** Positive - symptoms improved after the suspected medicine was stopped.

**Alternative explanations:** No newly introduced food, cosmetic product, insect exposure, antibiotic, or other medicine is reported in the fictional scenario.

## Preliminary conclusion
The event is consistent with a **potential diclofenac-associated hypersensitivity-type ADR**. The timing and positive dechallenge support the preliminary assessment, while the absence of rechallenge and the fictional nature of the scenario limit certainty.

## Why this case demonstrates Task 1 skills
1. Starts with the observed reaction.
2. Separates long-standing and newly introduced medicines.
3. Establishes a clear timeline.
4. Documents dechallenge and rechallenge status.
5. Considers alternative explanations.
6. Avoids claiming definitive causality from limited information.
